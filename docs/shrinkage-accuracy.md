# Shrinkage accuracy vs hidden truth

Generated by `uv run python -m formline.evaluation --truth-matches 300`.

**Question.** Does shrinkage give better player baselines than a player's raw average?

**Truth.** Each player's population mean strong-foot shot speed: the same league and
hidden profiles simulated for 300 extra matches (only seeds differ). Players
need 30+ truth shots to be included.

**Estimates** come from the 30-match history, as the engine sees it: *raw* (his own
average), *group* (his position group's average, leaving him out) and *shrunk* (the
engine's blend, weight n / (n + k)).

## Error vs truth (RMSE, km/h; lower is better)

| Sample in history | Players | Raw | Group | Shrunk | Error cut by shrinkage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All players | 39 | 4.11 | 5.03 | 2.87 | 30% |
| 1–4 shots | 6 | 6.26 | 3.05 | 3.45 | 45% |
| 5–9 shots | 11 | 5.41 | 4.94 | 3.91 | 28% |
| 10–19 shots | 15 | 2.14 | 5.91 | 1.84 | 14% |
| 20+ shots | 7 | 2.20 | 4.45 | 2.05 | 7% |

## Findings

- Shrinkage cuts the error of a raw average by 30% overall, and by 45% for players with 1–4 shots: the smaller the sample, the more it helps.
- For the smallest samples the plain group average does slightly better than the shrunk estimate: the history underestimates k (see below), so the engine under-shrinks a little. More history or a slightly stronger prior would fix it; the effect is small next to the gain over raw averages.
- The group average alone is the worst estimate overall: players really do differ, so a baseline must be personal.

## Shrinkage strength

k = shot-to-shot variance / player-to-player variance. *Fitted* is what the engine
estimated from the 30-match history; *implied* is computed from the truth sample.

| FWD | 2.2 | 3.9 |
